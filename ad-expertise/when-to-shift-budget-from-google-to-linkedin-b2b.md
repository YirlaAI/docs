When CPL goes up, the first question you get is usually "should we shift budget off LinkedIn?" or "are we spending too much on Google?" Neither question has a clean answer without knowing which job each channel is doing for your specific program.

Here's the framework that actually survives scrutiny. Google's CPC for B2B search runs $2 to $8, with conversion rates as high as 7.5% on bottom-funnel terms, because the searcher already identified their problem. LinkedIn's CPC runs $8 to $15+, but [GrowthSpree's account-level benchmark work](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/linkedin-ads-vs-google-ads-b2b-saas-where-to-spend-next-10k-2026) shows cost per company influenced often landing lower on LinkedIn once you measure at the account level, because a single LinkedIn campaign can reach five stakeholders at one target account where Google captured one individual searcher.

Use this decision table before shifting spend:

| Scenario | Move Budget Toward | Key Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Short sales cycle, buyers already searching | Google | Search volume on category/branded terms |
| Long enterprise cycle, named account list | LinkedIn | Cost per company influenced |
| ICP saturated on one channel | The other one | CPL trending up without targeting change |
| Unclear which is working | Neither — fix attribution first | Cost per company influenced unavailable |

Check this quarterly, not reactively. ICP shifts and competitive saturation change which channel is doing the heavier lifting on a faster cadence than most teams review.
