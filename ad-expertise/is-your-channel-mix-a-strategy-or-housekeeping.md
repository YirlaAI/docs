If the quarterly paid media deck you saw this week opened with "LinkedIn CPM up 18%, Google flat, recommend optimizing the mix," you watched someone mistake housekeeping for strategy. Optimization and strategy are different jobs, and most demand gen teams only run the first one.

A strategy conversation about channel mix opens with a harder question. Knowing what you know today about how your buyer actually behaves, where would you put the money if you were starting over tomorrow? The mix you already have then becomes the hypothesis on trial, not the floor the conversation has to stand on.

Most CMOs inherit a channel mix the way they inherit a CRM — set up by the last person in the seat, reinforced by the agency, quietly validated by whatever attribution model the team runs, and politely left alone for two or three years. When CPM ticks up, the reflex is to press the existing levers harder. You cannot press your way to a different mix. You have to step out of the optimization loop entirely.

Here is what keeps the mix frozen, side-by-side:

| Force | Why it stays stuck |
| --- | --- |
| Platform self-interest | LinkedIn will never tell you to move money to Google, and Google will never tell you to try Reddit. Each platform optimizes within its own walls. |
| Attribution validation | Most attribution models were built to justify the current mix. The output tends to confirm the input. |
| Loudest voices in the room | The people most vocal about channel performance are usually the ones whose jobs depend on the current mix. Not bad faith, just human nature. |

The move is to put something in the room that does not have a stake in the current mix. A single unified view across every channel, not another dashboard with five tabs, but one picture of where dollars are flowing, what they are generating, and what a 10% shift into Google or Reddit or programmatic video would actually look like. The channels are already running the experiment for you. The question is whether you are set up to read the result.

Gartner's annual CMO spend work keeps showing that digital ad budgets drift across categories every 12 to 18 months, even when the headline budget stays flat ([Gartner CMO Spend Survey](https://www.gartner.com/en/marketing/research/annual-cmo-spend-survey-research)). The $4.2M you spent last year tells a different story than the $4.2M you will spend next year, even if the split on the page looks identical.

At some point the CFO is going to walk into your office with a printout and ask why line six of the paid media budget has not moved since 2024. Do not answer that question with a performance report. Answer it with a decision and the reasoning behind it. That is where [a system of record for your paid media](https://www.yirla.com/en/platform) earns its seat in your stack.

Block 20 minutes with your team this quarter. Ask the harder question. If the answer lands at "roughly the same mix," fine — now you have a defensible position. If it lands at "we would do it differently," also fine — now you have a plan.

What you do not have, in either case, is a housekeeping problem.
