The best creative brief your team ever wrote probably showed up by accident, after somebody on your team saw an ad on a competitor's page that was working. The second-best one showed up a month later, after the same person noticed the competitor shift the angle and realized the first ad had stopped working.

You should be that person. Or you should become that person. The 30-minute Tuesday routine that makes it happen is the least glamorous skill that separates a demand gen practitioner from an ad operator.

The routine itself is boring on purpose. Do not overthink it. Open Meta Ad Library, LinkedIn Ads Transparency, and Google Ads Transparency in three tabs. Pick three competitors — not a long list, just three that matter most this quarter. Scan the last 30 days of active creatives for each. Screenshot three things per competitor, which is nine screenshots total. Paste them into a single running doc with a one-line note on each. That is the whole thing. Thirty minutes. One doc that grows over time. Nobody sees it but you, unless you want them to.

The three things to screenshot every time are the same three things, so you are looking for pattern shifts rather than hoarding volume:

| What to capture | What you are looking for |
| --- | --- |
| The angle | The hook in the first 2 seconds, or the first line of copy |
| The format | Static, video, carousel, document, testimonial |
| The landing experience | A single LP, a calculator, a webinar reg, an email capture |

What changes over three months is that you stop reacting to individual ads and start seeing the decisions behind them. Competitor A switched from "platform" messaging to "outcome" messaging in February. Competitor B killed all their long-form videos and went to 15-second testimonials in March. Competitor C stopped running on LinkedIn entirely in April. Each of those is a data point. Together, they are a pattern you can bring into your next planning meeting.

Your manager is going to notice, because you will walk into Wednesday stand-ups with ideas that came from outside the room. That is unusual, and unusual is career capital.

The uncomfortable truth is that the agency is not doing this for you. They are running the brief you gave them. If you want your brief to get better, you are the one who has to update it. This is the same logic that makes [a decision log](https://www.yirla.com/en-us/blog/the-decision-log-that-saves-your-job) valuable — external signal feeding internal discipline. HubSpot's State of Marketing report has noted year after year that in-house practitioners with a competitive review habit outperform those without one ([HubSpot State of Marketing](https://www.hubspot.com/state-of-marketing)).

Open the three tabs next Tuesday at 9am. Screenshot nine things. Paste them into a doc. Write one line on each. Close the tabs.

You will be better at your job in 90 days. You will be irreplaceable in 12 months. That is the whole pitch. No tool required to start.
