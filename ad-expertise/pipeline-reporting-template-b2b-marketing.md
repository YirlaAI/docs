A B2B pipeline reporting template needs three sections that answer three questions in order: how much pipeline did marketing contribute, how fast is it moving, and which channels are actually efficient. Build those three sections once as a live template and the monthly report stops being a rebuild-from-scratch spreadsheet exercise.

## What a Pipeline Report Needs to Answer for Leadership

Leadership isn't reading a pipeline report for the numbers themselves, they're reading it to answer three questions: is marketing contributing meaningfully to pipeline, is that pipeline moving at a healthy pace, and where should budget go next quarter. A report that doesn't answer those three questions directly, no matter how detailed, isn't doing its job.

## A Section-by-Section Template Breakdown

- **Section 1, Contribution:** total pipeline generated by marketing, broken out by sourced vs. influenced, with a trend line over the last six months, not just the current month in isolation.
- **Section 2, Velocity:** average days per stage for marketing-sourced pipeline vs. sales-sourced or overall, flagging any stage where marketing pipeline moves noticeably slower.
- **Section 3, Channel efficiency:** cost per opportunity and cost per closed-won by channel, ranked so the best and worst performers are immediately visible without hunting through a table.

## How Do You Source the Underlying Data Without Manual Exports?

Connect the report directly to CRM pipeline data and ad platform spend data rather than pulling CSVs each month. Even a simple live connection, refreshed weekly, eliminates the hour or two most marketing ops managers lose every month reconciling exports from three different systems into one spreadsheet.

## Adapting the Template for Board-Level vs. Team-Level Audiences

Board-level: three numbers per section, trend arrows, no raw tables. Team-level: full detail, campaign-by-campaign breakdowns, and the underlying data behind each trend line so the team can act on specifics, not just the headline. Same three sections, different depth. Building both from the same live data source means updating one doesn't mean rebuilding the other from scratch.

## FAQ

**What should a B2B pipeline report include?**
Pipeline contribution (sourced vs. influenced), velocity by stage, and channel efficiency, in that order.

**What's the difference between sourced and influenced pipeline?**
Sourced pipeline is deals marketing originated; influenced pipeline is any deal marketing touched at any point in the journey.

**How do you build a pipeline report without manual data exports?**
Connect the report directly to CRM and ad platform data sources rather than pulling and reconciling CSVs each month.

**Should board-level and team-level pipeline reports look different?**
Yes. Board-level should show three headline numbers per section with trend arrows; team-level should include full campaign detail.
