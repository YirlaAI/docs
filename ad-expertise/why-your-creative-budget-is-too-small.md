Open last year's annual paid media plan and look at the creative line. If it reads 18% of media, or somewhere close, you are looking at the single biggest reason your media efficiency has been drifting down for two years.

The 82/18 split between media and creative is the default. It is also the reason the line on your media efficiency chart keeps sloping the wrong way.

Creative is the highest-leverage line item in a paid media budget, and CMOs consistently underfund it because it does not show up in the dashboards the way media does. Media spend is easy to measure. Creative spend is easy to cut. So creative spend gets cut, the media spend that remains gets less and less efficient, and nobody connects the two.

Nielsen's long-running work on creative contribution to ROI arrives at the same answer over and over: creative is responsible for roughly half of paid media performance variation, and in some studies more ([Nielsen creative effectiveness research](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/)). Meta's own internal studies on ad system performance point to the same conclusion from a different angle. The creative is the lever. The spend is the amplifier.

If that is true, and the research has been consistent on this for a decade, then an 82/18 split is a strategic mistake. You are underfunding the lever and over-funding the amplifier. The hidden cost shows up as rising CPMs and falling CPOs, which read like platform problems but are actually creative problems that the platforms are charging you a premium to work around.

Three moves CMOs who are ahead of this have started making, in order:

First, moving the creative budget closer to 30% of paid media — not because of a formula but because that is where the leverage curve flattens. Second, hiring or contracting creative that iterates weekly rather than quarterly, so the lever is actually being pulled. Third, running creative and media under one P&L view instead of two, so the trade-offs are visible.

That third one is the CFO-facing move, and it is what unlocks the other two. As long as creative is budgeted separately, it will be treated as a discretionary cost. When it is pooled into paid media performance, it becomes a performance decision — which is the conversation you actually want to have.

The conversation to have with finance is not "I need more creative budget." That is a request. The conversation is "our media efficiency is declining and the research says creative is the primary lever, so I am moving the split from 82/18 to 70/30, and here is the test to validate it." That is a position, and positions get approved faster than requests.
