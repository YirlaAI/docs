The budget allocation question most CMOs are answering every quarter is the wrong one. "Which channel performs better" almost guarantees the wrong split, because it assumes LinkedIn and Google are competing for the same job.

They aren't. And the revenue data proves it.

[Fibbler's 2026 channel analysis](https://www.fibbler.co/blog/linkedin-ads-vs-google-ads-b2b-pipeline) found LinkedIn returning $1.21 in attributed revenue per dollar spent against Google's $0.67. Google's CPL is lower, often $8 to $15 versus $35 to $50 on LinkedIn. Cheaper leads, worse revenue. That single stat explains most B2B channel misallocation.

The framework that actually maps to how enterprise buyers behave:

| Channel | Job to Be Done | Right Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search | Capture buyers who are already searching for a solution | CPL, conversion rate |
| LinkedIn | Reach buying committees before they start searching | Cost per company influenced |

Judging both against CPL is like grading a marathon and a sprint on the same stopwatch. The sprint will win every time, and you'll underfund the thing that's building the next quarter's pipeline.

What account-level attribution actually looks like in practice is on [Yirla's use cases](https://www.yirla.com/use-cases) page.

Ask what each channel is for, and the budget split gets a lot easier to defend.
