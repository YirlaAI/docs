Budget erosion rarely announces itself. More often it looks like a campaign that "still performs fine" while quietly costing 30% more per lead than it did two months ago.

Frequency is the number behind that slow bleed, and it's the most overlooked metric in a paid media review. The data is consistent across [GrowthSpree's 2026 account-level frequency analysis](https://www.growthspreeofficial.com/blogs/linkedin-ads-company-level-frequency-capping-distribute-budget-target-accounts): once a B2B audience crosses roughly 4 impressions per member, performance degrades, and most LinkedIn campaigns blow past that threshold within the first two weeks because the audience is small and the budget isn't.

Here's why this lands on you and not just your demand gen team. Frequency fatigue doesn't show up as a dramatic failure. It shows up as a slow erosion in CTR and CPL that gets explained away as "seasonality" or "the algorithm." By the time it hits a board deck, you're three months into paying more for less, and nobody flagged it because nobody owns watching it.

Three questions to bring to your next paid media review:

1. Are we pulling frequency by campaign, not just account-level averages that hide creative-specific burnout?
2. Is our creative refresh cadence tied to a frequency threshold, or to a calendar date?
3. Which campaigns are running above 8 to 12 impressions per person per month with no corresponding performance lift?

Your best ad isn't dying because the creative got worse. It's dying because you kept showing it to the same 4,000 people until they stopped seeing it at all.
