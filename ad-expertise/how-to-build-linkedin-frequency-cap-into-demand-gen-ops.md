Every marketing operating system has a budget pacing rule. Almost none of them have a frequency rule, and that gap is where campaigns quietly die between reporting cycles.

The frequency thresholds worth building into your system:

| Range | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Below 3 impressions/month | You haven't registered yet |
| 5–8 impressions/month | Sweet spot for awareness and consideration |
| 9–12 impressions/month | Monitor closely; creative fatigue starting |
| Above 12 impressions/month | You're paying to annoy people who've already ignored the ad twice |

[Stackmatix's 2026 cost guide](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/linkedin-ads-cost-budget-guide-2026) backs this range up across verticals, and it tracks with what most demand gen teams discover the hard way around month three of a campaign.

The operating system fix is straightforward: treat frequency the way you already treat budget pacing, a number you check weekly with a trigger, not a number you check when someone asks why CTR dropped. Add it to the same reporting cadence as your segment strategy review. If a segment's frequency climbs past 8 with flat or declining CTR, rotate creative or narrow audience before account-based teams downstream feel the pinch.
