Pull up the Meta Ad Library right now and search your largest competitor. You will see 30 to 40 active creatives, every format they are running, every angle they are testing. All public. All free. Nobody on your team has looked at any of it in the last six weeks.

That is not a tooling gap. That is a workflow gap, and it is a CMO-level issue because the people who should be running the analysis are not the people who have the authority to act on it.

The Meta Ad Library, the LinkedIn Ads Transparency Center, and the Google Ads Transparency Center are all public. Every major ad platform shipped one in the last three years under regulatory pressure, and the side effect is that the competitive intelligence problem got solved at the platform layer — but nobody updated the workflow on the marketing side to take advantage of it. The data is sitting there. Most teams have not built a habit around it.

A competitive ad intelligence practice should be answering three questions for you as CMO every quarter. First, what angles and messaging are our top five competitors running, and what shifted from last quarter? Second, which creative formats are they betting on, which ones are they killing quickly, and what does that tell us about what is working? Third, are any competitors entering or exiting channels, and if so, is that a signal for us? None of those are junior analyst questions. They are strategy questions. A junior analyst can do the data pull. The interpretation has to come from someone who understands positioning and has permission to act on it. That is usually you.

The structural reason teams ignore this work is that it does not belong to anyone. The agency will not do it unless you pay extra, because it is not campaign execution. The demand gen team is busy running campaigns and will not block time for work that does not ladder to this quarter's pipeline number. The analyst team treats it as a one-off research project, not a recurring rhythm. So the data stays public and unused.

Forrester's competitive intelligence maturity research points to the exact same gap. Most B2B marketing teams rate themselves as "ad hoc" on competitive monitoring, not systematic ([Forrester competitive intelligence research](https://www.forrester.com/research)). The tools exist. The habit does not.

What to ask of your team this quarter is not a report. It is a recurring review. Thirty minutes every month, five competitors, three questions per competitor, captured in a living doc. If you do not have the operational bandwidth internally, an [orchestrated workflow across your paid media stack](https://www.yirla.com/en/platform) can handle the pull and surface the interpretation question to you — which is the part you actually want to spend time on.

The competitors who are outworking you on positioning are the ones who have already built this habit. You can see their creative iteration speed from the outside. It is faster than yours. They know it. You should too.

Block 30 minutes on the calendar this month and go look at the ad libraries yourself. What you see there will change what you ask your team for next week.
