Every demo you sat through this quarter claimed the tool gives you answers. Very few of them give you judgment. The gap between those two things is the difference between a feature and a new operating model — and most CMOs have not been shown the distinction clearly.

Marketing AI right now lives at three tiers:

| Tier | What it does | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval | Finds something already in your stack faster than you could have found it yourself. | Faster teams. |
| Memory | Remembers what you asked last week or last quarter and can refer back. Claude and the newer AI assistants lean hard into this. | Continuity. Institutional memory on your computer. |
| Synthesis | Takes signals across channels, time horizons, and data sources, then produces a judgment you would otherwise have formed over three meetings and four dashboards. | A different operating model. Compresses "surface data" to "make a call" from weeks to minutes. |

McKinsey's work on AI in enterprise marketing points to the same split. Productivity gains come from retrieval and memory; competitive advantage comes from synthesis ([McKinsey AI in marketing](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/growth-marketing-and-sales/our-insights)). CMOs who buy tier-one tools get faster teams. CMOs who buy tier-three tools get different outcomes.

The question to ask a vendor is not "does your product use AI." That is a box-checking question, and every vendor will say yes. The harder question: can your tool look at six weeks of paid media performance across four channels and tell me what I should move, why, and what happens if I do not? If the answer is a prettier dashboard, it is tier one. If the answer is "here is what the data suggests and here is why," it is tier three.

Your CFO should care because tier three is the only tier that touches the capital allocation question. Retrieval makes a $4M paid media budget easier to report on. Synthesis makes a $4M paid media budget easier to defend.

For the demand gen team, the practical impact is the difference between walking into a QBR with performance data and walking in with a recommended move. One of those is a status update. The other is a strategy conversation. We go deeper on this distinction on [the Yirla platform page](https://www.yirla.com/en/platform).

The CMO question for Q2 is not "should we adopt more AI." That ship sailed. The question is whether the AI in your stack is actually making judgments, or just making retrieval look like judgment. One of those is worth the spend. The other is an invoice.
