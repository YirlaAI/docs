I use Claude Cowork every day. I pay for it. It is also not the thing keeping my campaigns honest.

If you are the person who actually runs the campaigns, lives in LinkedIn Campaign Manager, pulls the Looker dashboards, and stitches CRM data into a messy spreadsheet on Friday afternoon, you already know the operational cost of "just ask Claude." It is real work:

- export the data;
- clean it;
- paste it in;
- frame the question;
- read the answer;
- act on it;
- repeat on Monday.

That is a fine workflow if you are doing it once. If you are doing it for 30 campaigns across two platforms with creative refreshes every 14 days, the workflow is the bottleneck.

Here is what is actually different between a general-purpose AI and a purpose-built paid media system:

| Dimension | Claude (general-purpose) | Purpose-built system |
|-----------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Data pipeline | Needs you to bring the data | Pulls it itself |
| Memory | Forgets when the session ends | Carries history |
| Framework | Reasons fresh each time | Already knows what saturation, fatigue, and drift look like in your account |
| Trigger | Waits for you to ask | Watches without being asked |
| Audit | Answer vanishes into a chat log | Flagged decisions live in a tracked record |

None of this is a knock on Claude. Claude is a brilliant tool. But brilliant tools need brilliant operators who have the time to operate them. Anyone currently managing a paid media budget knows the constraint is not brainpower, it is reps per week.

One stat worth holding onto: data silos alone account for roughly 30% of average B2B ad spend waste, per published benchmarks this year ([FL0, 2026 Benchmark Study](https://fl0.com/blog/wasted-b2b-ad-spend-benchmark-study-2026)). You can fix silos by paying a brilliant generalist to reason about your data on demand, which is expensive and slow, or you can fix them by connecting the pipes once and letting the system do the watching. The second is what Yirla is.

So when someone on my team asks "can't I just use Claude for this?", the answer is yes and no. Yes for the novel problem that needs fresh reasoning. No for the standing operational responsibility of running paid media without burning 30 to 40% of the budget on noise.
