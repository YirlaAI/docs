Annual planning offsites produce two things: a channel mix, and eleven months of defending it. The offsite is not the enemy. The defense posture is. In 2026, a static mix is a bet that your buyer has stopped changing, and your buyer has not stopped changing.

LinkedIn is where they read. Reddit is where they complain. Google is where they research. A meaningful share of them are forming pre-vendor opinions inside an LLM before any of those other things happen ([Forrester B2B buyer research](https://www.forrester.com/research/)). Your reallocation cadence needs to match the speed your buyer is moving at, not the cadence your finance team likes.

The teams that win in this environment run a 90-day channel mix test. Not a bet-the-quarter one. A small, disciplined reallocation tested against the same pipeline target, reviewed every 30 days, with explicit stop conditions and clear outcome deltas.

The cadence in a table:

| Month | Move |
| --- | --- |
| Month 0 | Pick one over-indexed channel and one you have never taken seriously. Shift 10% of budget. Lock the period. |
| Month 1 | Input metrics only. Do not touch output metrics yet. This is a patience problem more than an analytics problem. |
| Month 2 | Compare pipeline influence and cost-per-opportunity across the shift. Annotate anything that moved for reasons other than the reallocation. |
| Month 3 | Make the call. Keep, expand, or roll back — and write down why. |

The "write down why" row is the only one that matters on a two-year horizon. Every team that runs real reallocation has a decision log. Most teams that do not have a dashboard.

What makes 90-day tests hard is not the math. It is the coordination. Your agency built its slide template around the current mix. Your attribution model points at the current mix. Your QBRs are set up to explain the current mix. A reallocation touches all of it. That is why most teams never run one.

The move is making reallocation a process, not a political act. When the monthly channel review is already on the calendar — same five questions, same data, same record of what you tried last quarter — it stops being a negotiation and starts being a discipline. That is the whole point of running [a system of record for your paid media decisions](https://www.yirla.com/en/platform) rather than a quarterly recount.

You do not need to be right. You need to be able to show, three quarters from now, that you ran four reallocation tests, two worked, two did not, and here is what you learned from each. That is what a real answer to "what is your channel strategy?" sounds like. Not "LinkedIn 60, Google 25, Meta 10, other 5." A track record of tested moves and their outcomes.

If you cannot describe your last four channel mix tests, you do not have a channel strategy. You have a budget. Start the first one next Monday.
