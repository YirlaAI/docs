The campaign doesn't fail all at once. CTR slides 15% over two weeks, CPL drifts up without a targeting change, and the ad that crushed it in week one is solidly mediocre by week four. You've seen this before. The question is whether you catch it early this time.

[SingleGrain's frequency capping breakdown](https://www.singlegrain.com/abm/linkedin-abm-frequency-capping-avoiding-ad-fatigue/) and the 2026 benchmark consensus align on the same window: B2B audiences hit creative fatigue between 8 and 12 monthly impressions. LinkedIn's addressable B2B pools are small to begin with, which means you reach that ceiling faster than you'd expect, especially on tightly defined ABM lists.

Here's the operational sequence for catching it early:

1. Pull frequency by campaign weekly, not monthly. By the time a monthly report flags it, you've already overpaid for three weeks.
2. Rotate creative on a fixed 3 to 4 week schedule instead of waiting for performance to tell you it's time.
3. Segment your highest-value accounts into a separate campaign with a lower frequency cap. Burning out a named account list costs more than burning out a broad audience.
4. Track CPL and CTR together. CPL can hold steady through early CTR decline until it suddenly doesn't, and by then the damage is done.

This is the same instinct behind [Three Signals That Say You Should Move Budget Off LinkedIn](https://www.yirla.com/en-us/blog/three-signals-move-budget-off-linkedin): fatigue isn't always a reason to kill a channel, it's usually a reason to rebuild a specific campaign. Catching this early isn't glamorous. It's just the difference between a campaign that ages well and one that drains budget for a month before anyone notices.
