If you run a LinkedIn account and have ever been asked "how is it performing?" and said "roughly the same," that is the signal. Not the answer — the phrase itself. "Roughly the same" is what a slow leak sounds like when you describe it out loud.

The platform is built to make that feel fine. CPM crept up 4% again. CTR is holding. CPL is stable if you squint. The quarterly deck still renders green. Meanwhile, the pipeline is not growing, and you — the person actually running the account — are the first one to notice, and the one with the least political cover to say it out loud.

Here are the three signals that give you the ammunition.

The first is your frequency cap doing most of the work. When a frequency cap starts quietly eating 12 to 18% of your theoretical impressions, LinkedIn is telling you the audience is saturated. You can keep paying full freight for diminishing unique reach, or you can acknowledge that the next dollar would go further somewhere else. Audience overlap climbing for two quarters running is not a creative problem. It is a channel problem. We walked through an adjacent version of this in ["Your Campaigns Are Fine. Your System Is Broken."](https://www.yirla.com/en-us/blog/your-campaigns-are-fine.-your-system-is-broken)

The second is incremental cost-per-opportunity flat against climbing spend. This is the chart nobody wants to run. Pull your last four quarters of LinkedIn spend and plot it against net-new opportunities created. Not total. Not influenced. Created. If that line is horizontal or drifting down while spend is climbing, you are buying air. LinkedIn's own research shows B2B CPMs rose sharply year-over-year in 2025 ([LinkedIn Marketing Solutions benchmarks](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/b2b-benchmark)). If pipeline is not moving with that, you are losing real dollars to inflation.

The third is a competitor set running creative you would normally dismiss — and it is working. If a competitor started running plain-text asset ads, native documents, or spoken-in-English testimonial cards and engagement is climbing, the platform is telling you the creative rules have shifted. If your instinct is "we would never run that," you are probably already behind. The question is not whether to copy; the question is whether your creative team even saw it.

One signal is a check-in. Two is a meeting. Three is a budget conversation.

The move is not to drop LinkedIn. LinkedIn is still where most B2B buyers get oriented on your category. The move is to hold a LinkedIn baseline and test the next 10 to 15% of your budget somewhere else for one quarter. Google. Reddit. YouTube. Depends on your buyer.

You are not abandoning the channel. You are refusing to keep paying full freight for diminishing reach while a competitor is quietly finding a cheaper place to stand.

Run the math next Monday. If the signals are there, you already know what to do.
