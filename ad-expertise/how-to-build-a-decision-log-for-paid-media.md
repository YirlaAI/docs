Picture the QBR where the CMO asks why Q2 LinkedIn spend was flat while Google was up 22%, and the decision you need to cite was made in a Slack thread eleven weeks ago that nobody can find. If you run the account, that QBR is coming for you.

The dashboard in front of everyone shows the what. It does not show the why. You improvise, you mostly survive, and on the walk back to your desk you think, "I need a better system." You do. And it is simpler than you think.

What protects you in that QBR is not a better dashboard. It is a decision log. Not a Notion wiki, not a project plan — a running log of every meaningful call your team made, with a one-liner on why. One row per decision, four columns:

- Date;
- The call — what changed, where, how much;
- The reason — hypothesis in one sentence;
- The review trigger — the metric or date that would make you revisit;

That is it. The discipline is in keeping it, not in the template.

Here is what changes the next time you walk into a review with that log open instead of a performance deck. You stop defending numbers, which is a losing game, because numbers can always be framed unfavorably. You start walking through decisions, which is a winning game, because decisions carry context the numbers do not. A sentence like "we paused branded search on April 3 because organic was cannibalizing it, review trigger was incremental click volume at 60 days, we checked on June 2, the numbers held, the pause stayed" is not an opinion. It is a record.

Start the log this Friday. Back-fill the last four weeks of meaningful decisions. It will take 45 minutes, and it will be oddly therapeutic, because you will realize you actually did make good calls — you just did not write them down.

The Content Marketing Institute has been flagging this for years ([CMI marketing ops research](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/research/)). The teams that build durable credibility with the CMO and the CFO are not the ones with the prettiest dashboards. They are the ones who can tell a coherent story about why the money moved the way it did.

Once the habit is real, you can decide whether you need a tool to run it at scale, or whether a shared doc is enough. That is a question for later. Right now you need the habit. The log is not busy work. The log is what gets you into the next budget conversation with a seat at the table instead of a screenshot to defend.
