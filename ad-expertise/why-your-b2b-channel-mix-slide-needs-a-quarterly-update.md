Channel mix slides get built in January and defended for the next eleven months. That's not a strategy, it's inertia with a logo on it, and it's why the LinkedIn-versus-Google debate resurfaces every quarter without ever getting resolved.

The 2026 industry baseline sits around 41% LinkedIn and 46% Google Network. That's a reasonable starting point, not a destination. [Stackmatix's comparison data](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/linkedin-vs-google-ads-b2b-lead-gen) shows the split shifts materially based on a few variables:

- Short sales cycles and lower ACV tend to favor Google's intent capture
- Enterprise cycles with 4+ buying committee members tend to favor LinkedIn's account-level reach
- High ICP saturation on either platform is a reason to rebalance regardless of prior year allocation

Building this into your marketing operating system means the channel mix slide becomes a quarterly deliverable tied to your segment strategy, reviewed against actual pipeline data, not against a benchmark deck from the prior year. The question to bring to that review isn't "what's our CPL by channel," it's "what's our cost per company influenced by channel," since that's the metric that reflects how buying committees actually work.

The same framework drives [Three Signals That Say You Should Move Budget Off LinkedIn](https://www.yirla.com/en-us/blog/three-signals-move-budget-off-linkedin): the right call is rarely to abandon a channel, it's to rebalance based on what the current quarter's account-level data shows. If your reporting can't answer cost per company influenced today, [Yirla's pricing page](https://www.yirla.com/pricing) shows what building that capability actually looks like.

Update the slide every quarter and the LinkedIn-versus-Google debate mostly resolves itself.
